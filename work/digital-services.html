03 / Municipal website and facility requests

↑ All work

What happens after a resident submits a form?

Until October 2026, the only way to reserve a Spring Valley park, community center, or the portable stage was a paper application picked up at Village Hall. I reorganized how residents find village forms, then set up and tested online requests for seven facilities inside the website platform the Village already had.

My role
Led the content audit, Forms Index, request flow, and the EvoGov setup and testing
Worked with
The Village Clerk and departments (requirements); the EvoGov vendor (platform limits)
When

Outcome
Forms Index live; online requests live for seven facilities

Online payment (awaiting approval); usage data

Where residents got stuck

Spring Valley’s website was organized the way the government is organized, by department. Forms, payment links, and instructions sat on different department pages, so a resident had to know which office handled something before they could start. Even then, a form didn’t always tell you how to finish the task.

Facility rentals were the clearest example, because there was no online option at all. A resident went to Village Hall, picked up a paper application, filled it out, came back with everything, and paid at the Clerk’s office. From my review of how requests actually ran, that usually meant two or three trips, sometimes four, plus about two phone calls per request.

Following the request behind the counter

I sat with the Clerk’s office, interviewed staff, and went through the major permit requests to see what happened after an application came in. The resident’s side was simple: pick a place and a time, and find out what happens next. The Clerk’s side was a checklist done by hand. Before anything could be confirmed, someone had to check the date, the facility’s rules, the applicant’s insurance, and the fee. An online version had to work for that person too.

In parallel, I audited the site’s forms and navigation and gathered the forms into a single Forms Index, still grouped under the department that owns each one. I also reworked the homepage so the common tasks (reporting a problem, finding a form, making a payment) were easier to reach.

Working inside the platform

The version I wanted was simple: pick the place, request the time, and see exactly what happens next. EvoGov, the Village’s content management system, had limits I couldn’t design around. Its booking portal requires residents to log in. Its staff permissions couldn’t be narrowed enough for some of the people who needed to see requests. It couldn’t enforce every rule from the paper forms, and it didn’t support the seasonal sunset cutoffs we asked for.

I could have pushed for a custom tool. I used the native features instead, because staff could operate them and I could test them properly, and I documented the limitations.

A request, not a reservation

I set up seven facilities and resources for online requests: the Louis Kurtz Center, the Darden Center, the Memorial Park soccer field and basketball courts, Kennedy Park, Gould Park, and the portable stage. Each request moves through four states (requested, approved, confirmed, cancelled), and the public page says plainly that a request isn’t approved until the Village confirms it. I didn’t want the page to imply a reservation the Clerk hadn’t agreed to.

I tested the setup with controlled requests, taking records from new to confirmed to cancelled and checking that the public calendar reserved and then released the slot, including around holidays. The application forms also went online as PDFs, which saves residents at least the first trip.

What’s live and what isn’t

The Forms Index and online requests for all seven resources are live. Online payment, requests without an account, and finer-grained staff permissions are not. I’ve designed the next version: a resident submits without creating an account, the Clerk approves, the resident pays online after approval, and one reference number follows the request to the end. That design is waiting on approval, and the payment integration hasn’t been verified.

There’s no usage or completion data yet, so I can’t say how many residents have used the new process or how many trips it has saved. What the project did show me is that the page residents see is only part of the job. A simpler experience for residents holds up only if staff can receive the request, see where it stands, and carry it through.
